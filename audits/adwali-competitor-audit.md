# Competitor Audit — adwali (adwali.com / IG @adwali.co)

_Prepared for internal strategy. Goal: learn what's working for a newer competitor
and decide what to adopt / beat._

> **Data-confidence note:** adwali.com is blocked by our network egress, so I could
> **not crawl the live site** (no first-hand check of page speed, on-page SEO tags,
> full sitemap, or exact page count). Findings below are built from search-engine
> snippets, their indexed pages, and their public Instagram (the post we already
> had). Website + technical-SEO sections are **inferred, not verified** — flagged
> where relevant. Everything should be re-checked once we can open the site.

---

## 1. Snapshot
- **What they are:** a small, new **Fashion & Beauty** marketing agency, Jaipur-based.
- **One-liner (theirs):** make fashion/beauty brands *"impossible to scroll past"* —
  bold creative + influencer buzz + paid social, "all tied to real results."
- **Services:** SEO, ads, social, web — **one team**; visual content, creator
  partnerships, paid campaigns, brand identity, e-commerce optimization.
- **Positioning hooks:** *"lean overheads… without the big-agency markup"* (value/price),
  and a founder story about being *"tired of marketing that looked busy but changed nothing."*
- **Maturity:** early-stage. Tiny search footprint, tiny social following, few indexed pages.

---

## 2. Positioning & messaging — **their biggest strength**
- **Niched down to one vertical (Fashion & Beauty).** This is the smart move. A
  specific "we do X for Y" beats a generalist "digital marketing agency" every time —
  easier to rank, easier to refer, higher trust with the target buyer.
- **Anti-"busy marketing" founder narrative** = a clear enemy + a promise ("results,
  not activity"). Memorable and repeatable.
- **Price/value angle** ("without the big-agency markup") lowers the barrier for SMB
  fashion/beauty brands.
- **Tone:** confident, witty, Hinglish-friendly (IG tagline *"Har brand ki adwali baat"*),
  meme-literate.

## 3. Website  *(inferred — not crawled)*
- Appears small and benefit-led, with an **industry landing page**
  (`/industry-fashion-beauty/`) and **testimonials** on-site (3 short client quotes
  surfaced in search).
- Likely a handful of pages (home, industry, services, contact). Could not verify tech
  stack, speed, mobile, or on-page tags.
- **Testimonials are generic** ("they get results," "easy to work with") — **no hard
  numbers, no named case studies.** That's a gap (see §7).

## 4. SEO — **weak / early**
- **Very thin index:** a `site:adwali.com` search returns essentially **one real page**
  (the fashion-beauty industry page); the rest of the SERP is noise (villages named
  "Adnawali/Alewali/…").
- **Brand-name confusion:** "adwali" collides with (a) those village names and (b) a
  *different* agency **"ADWALA" (@adwalaa.in)**. Their branded search is muddy.
- **New domain → low authority.** No evidence of a content engine, blog, or backlinks.
- **The one thing they did right:** an **industry-specific landing page** — a template
  that's easy to scale into programmatic SEO (industry × service × city).

## 5. Socials — **small but on-brand**
- Instagram **@adwali.co**; engagement is tiny (the post we have: ~4 likes, 2 shares) —
  a new account still building.
- Content is **witty, single-idea, pun-driven** (e.g., the "Term SEO" clove pun) with a
  consistent cream/red palette, a bold headline, and one clear CTA ("GET IN TOUCH NOW")
  plus a contact bar. **Strong creative craft, low distribution/consistency so far.**
- No meaningful LinkedIn presence found.

## 6. Creatives & communication — **the part that impressed us**
- **Signature look:** cream background, heavy black headline, one red accent word,
  hand-drawn marker squiggles, a single hero object, a yellow CTA pill.
- **Formula:** one witty idea per post → bold hook → single CTA → branded contact bar.
- **Why it works:** scroll-stopping, instantly on-brand, low cognitive load.
- **Weakness:** cleverness without proof. Puns get a smile; they don't prove ROI.

---

## 7. SWOT (for us, about them)
**Strengths:** clear niche, sharp positioning + founder story, strong witty creative,
value pricing, full-stack "one team."
**Weaknesses:** near-zero SEO footprint, brand-name confusion, tiny social reach,
**no hard proof / numbers / named case studies**, thin site.
**Opportunities (for them):** content engine, more industry/city pages, real case studies.
**Threats (to us):** their creative bar is high and their niche story is tight — if they
add proof + consistency, they become dangerous.

---

## 8. What WE should implement (prioritized)

**A. Lead with PROOF — this is our moat.**
adwali sells cleverness; we can sell **cleverness + receipts.** We already have real,
redacted dashboards (22x, 45x months, ₹3L→₹68L). They have generic testimonials and no
numbers. **Put the proof carousel + results card front-and-centre** on site, LinkedIn,
and IG. This is the single biggest gap we can exploit.

**B. Niche the story (without losing range).**
Their specificity beats a generic "digital marketing agency." Lead with our strongest,
provable vertical — **performance marketing for D2C / e-commerce** — as the headline
positioning, even if we serve others. Consider **industry landing pages** (D2C,
e-commerce, [your verticals]) mirroring their `/industry-…/` play, but each backed by
*real results* they can't match.

**C. Match the creative bar, keep our system.**
Their posts win on one witty idea + one CTA. Our charcoal/lime system already looks
premium — add **more wit / puns / Hinglish** to the hooks so we're scroll-stopping AND
credible. Keep the locked template so every post is unmistakably ours.

**D. Out-SEO them (low bar).**
Their index is ~1 page. A basic content engine wins: an **industry × service** page set +
a few genuinely useful articles + a clean, fast site + owning our branded SERP. Their
brand name is muddy; make ours crisp.

**E. Steal the good positioning lines, upgrade them.**
- Them: "results, not activity." → Us: "results, with the screenshots to prove it."
- Them: "without the big-agency markup." → Us: "senior media buying, not a junior and a
  dashboard." (or your real edge)
- Them: founder story about wasteful marketing → Us: founder story **anchored to a real
  22x account.**

**F. Win on consistency + distribution.**
Their reach is small because output is sporadic. We already built the scheduler + a
20-post content bank + hook cheat sheet. **Just shipping consistently beats them.**

---

## 9. One-line takeaway
adwali has the **craft and the niche**; they lack the **proof and the consistency.**
We have the proof and the systems — so the winning move is: **their creative bar + our
real numbers + relentless consistency.**
